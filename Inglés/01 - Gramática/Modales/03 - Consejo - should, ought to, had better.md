<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">`should`, `ought to` y `had better` sirven para dar consejo o recomendar</mark>. Son la base de muchas respuestas de speaking ("What should I do?").

# Los tres, de menor a mayor fuerza

- **`should` / `shouldn't`** — el consejo neutro, el más frecuente: *You **should** see a doctor. You **shouldn't** eat so much sugar.*
- **`ought to`** — igual que `should`, más formal y menos común (negativa/pregunta raras): *You **ought to** apologise.*
- **`had better`** (`'d better`) — consejo **urgente**, con <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">aviso de consecuencia negativa si no se hace</mark>: *You**'d better** leave now or you'll miss the train.*

# Detalles de forma

- `had better` va con **infinitivo sin `to`**: *You'd better **go*** (no *to go*). Negativa: *You**'d better not** be late.*
- Pedir consejo: *What **should** I do? Do you think I **should**…?*
- `should` también expresa **expectativa/probabilidad**: *She **should** be home by now* (= es de esperar que).

# Reproche y arrepentimiento sobre el pasado

<mark style="background: #8000E1A6;">`should have + participio` = lo que habría sido correcto hacer y no se hizo</mark> (crítica o arrepentimiento): *You **should have told** me! I **shouldn't have eaten** so much.*

# Casos atípicos

- `had better` **no es pasado** pese al `had`: habla del **presente/futuro**.
- `ought to` en negativa/interrogativa suena forzado; se prefiere `should`.

> [!warning]+ Fallos típicos
> - `should to`: *You should to rest* ❌ → *You should **rest*** ✅.
> - Confundir *had better* con pasado: *I had better go now* = ahora, no ayer.
> - `must` en lugar de `should` para un consejo suave: *must* es obligación, `should` es recomendación.

> [!warning]+ Trampas de examen ("para pillar")
> - **`had better` + base sin to**, y negativa **`had better not`** (no *hadn't better*).
> - **`should have + participio`** para el pasado: reproche/arrepentimiento.

# Ejercicios

> [!question]+ 1. Rellena (should/shouldn't/had better)
> 1. You look ill. You ______ go home.
> 2. We ______ hurry, or we'll be late. (urgente)
> 3. He ______ spend so much money. (consejo negativo)
> 4. "I've got a headache." "You ______ take an aspirin."

> [!success]- Solución 1
> 1. **should** — 2. **had better** ('d better) — 3. **shouldn't** — 4. **should / ought to**

> [!question]+ 2. Presente o pasado (should / should have)
> 1. You ______ (study) more for the last exam; you failed.
> 2. You ______ (drink) more water; it's hot today.
> 3. I ______ (not/say) that yesterday. I'm sorry.

> [!success]- Solución 2
> 1. **should have studied** — 2. **should drink** — 3. **shouldn't have said**

> [!question]+ 3. Corrige el error
> 1. You had better to see a doctor.
> 2. We hadn't better wait here.
> 3. You should to call her.

> [!success]- Solución 3
> 1. You had better **see** a doctor — 2. We**'d better not** wait here — 3. You should **call** her

> [!question]+ 4. ¿Cuál es la frase CORRECTA?
> 1. (a) You should have told me. (b) You should told me. (c) You should have to tell me.
> 2. (a) He'd better not drives. (b) He'd better not drive.

> [!success]- Solución 4
> 1. **a** — 2. **b**
