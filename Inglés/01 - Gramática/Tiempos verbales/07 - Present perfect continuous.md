<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">El *present perfect continuous* enfatiza la **duración** de una actividad que empezó en el pasado y sigue (o acaba de parar) con efecto visible ahora</mark>.

# Estructura

**have / has + been + verbo-`ing`**. *I**'ve been working**. She**'s been running**. Have you **been waiting** long?*

# Simple vs continuous

- **Continuous → el proceso / la duración:** *I**'ve been studying** all day* (énfasis en cuánto tiempo).
- **Simple → el resultado / la cantidad terminada:** *I**'ve studied** three units* (énfasis en lo logrado).
- **Resultado reciente visible:** *You're out of breath — **have** you **been running**? It**'s been raining** (el suelo está mojado).*

Se usa mucho con `for`, `since`, `all day`, `lately`, `recently` y **`How long…?`**.

# La distinción fina

<mark style="background: #8000E1A6;">Con verbos de acción larga (work, study, wait, rain), el continuous mira el proceso; el simple, el balance</mark>. A veces ambos valen con matiz distinto: *I've painted the kitchen* (terminado, resultado) vs *I've been painting the kitchen* (por eso estoy manchado, énfasis en la actividad).

# Casos atípicos

- **No con stative verbs:** *I've been knowing him for years* ❌ → *I**'ve known** him for years* ✅. Para estados que duran, **perfect simple**.
- **Verbos "puntuales"** (find, start, stop, break) tampoco encajan en la lógica de duración: usa el simple.

> [!warning]+ Trampas de examen ("para pillar")
> - **`How long have you been -ing?`** para actividades; con estados (know, have, be) → perfect simple.
> - Distinguir **"cuánto llevas" (continuous)** de **"cuánto has hecho" (simple)**: *How long have you been reading?* vs *How many pages have you read?*

# Ejercicios

> [!question]+ 1. Rellena con el present perfect continuous
> 1. How long ______ you ______ (learn) English?
> 2. My eyes hurt because I ______ (read) all day.
> 3. She's tired — she ______ (work) since eight.
> 4. It ______ (rain) for hours; the streets are flooded.

> [!success]- Solución 1
> 1. **have** you **been learning** — 2. **have been reading** — 3. **has been working** — 4. **has been raining**

> [!question]+ 2. Elige: perfect simple o continuous
> 1. I ______ (know) them for ten years.
> 2. I ______ (write) emails all morning — I'm exhausted.
> 3. She ______ (finish) three reports today.

> [!success]- Solución 2
> 1. **have known** (stative) — 2. **have been writing** (duración) — 3. **has finished** (cantidad terminada)

> [!question]+ 3. ¿Correcta (✔) o incorrecta (✘)?
> 1. I've been knowing her since school.
> 2. He's been playing football for two hours.
> 3. How long have you been having this car?

> [!success]- Solución 3
> 1. ✘ → I**'ve known** her — 2. ✔ — 3. ✘ → How long have you **had** this car? (have = posesión)

> [!question]+ 4. Traduce
> 1. Llevo esperando media hora.
> 2. Ha estado estudiando toda la tarde.

> [!success]- Solución 4
> 1. **I've been waiting for half an hour.** — 2. **She/He has been studying all afternoon.**
