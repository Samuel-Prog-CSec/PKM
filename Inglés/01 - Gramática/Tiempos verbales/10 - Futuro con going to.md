<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">`be going to` expresa planes e intenciones que ya tenías, y predicciones basadas en evidencia que ves ahora</mark>.

# Estructura

**am / is / are + going to + verbo base**. *I**'m going to study** tonight. It**'s going to rain**. **Are** you **going to** call her?*

# Cuándo se usa

- **Plan / intención previa:** *We**'re going to visit** my parents this weekend* (ya decidido).
- **Predicción con evidencia presente:** *Look at those clouds — it**'s going to rain**.* (la evidencia está a la vista).

# Contraste con will

| going to | will |
| --- | --- |
| Plan decidido antes de hablar | Decisión en el momento |
| Predicción con **evidencia visible** | Predicción como opinión/creencia |
| *I**'m going to** paint my room* | *I think I**'ll** paint it blue* |

<mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">La evidencia presente es la señal más fiable de `going to`</mark>: si en la frase hay algo que se ve/oye ahora y anuncia el futuro (*She's driving too fast — she's going to crash*), es `going to`.

# Casos atípicos

- **`was/were going to`** = plan pasado que no se cumplió: *I **was going to** call you, but I forgot.*
- Con los verbos **go** y **come**, en vez de *going to go/come* se prefiere el **present continuous**: *I**'m going** to the party* (mejor que *I'm going to go to the party*).

> [!warning]+ Fallos típicos
> - Olvidar el verbo *to be*: *I going to study* ❌ → *I**'m** going to study* ✅.
> - Usar `will` para un plan claramente previo suena raro: *Tonight I will meet my friends* (ya organizado) → *I**'m meeting / going to meet***.

> [!warning]+ Trampas de examen ("para pillar")
> - **Evidencia visible → going to** (no will): *Watch out! You**'re going to** fall!*
> - **"Ya lo había decidido" → going to**; **"lo decido ahora" → will**.

# Ejercicios

> [!question]+ 1. Rellena con going to
> 1. Look out! You ______ (fall)!
> 2. They ______ (get) married in June; they've booked everything.
> 3. "Why are you wearing boots?" "Because I ______ (walk) in the mountains."
> 4. I've decided: I ______ (learn) to drive this year.

> [!success]- Solución 1
> 1. **are going to fall** — 2. **are going to get** — 3. **am going to walk** — 4. **am going to learn**

> [!question]+ 2. Elige: will o going to
> 1. This box is heavy. — Don't worry, I ______ help you. → (a) 'll (b) 'm going to
> 2. She's studied hard; she ______ pass. → (a) will (b) is going to (evidencia)
> 3. I ______ visit my aunt on Sunday (plan). → (a) will (b) 'm going to

> [!success]- Solución 2
> 1. **a** — 2. **b** — 3. **b**

> [!question]+ 3. Corrige el error
> 1. I going to travel this summer.
> 2. Look! The glass is going to fall — will you catch it?
> 3. He is going to go to the cinema tonight.

> [!success]- Solución 3
> 1. I**'m** going to travel… — 2. correcto (aviso: predicción con evidencia + petición) — 3. mejor *He**'s going** to the cinema* (present continuous con *go*)

> [!question]+ 4. Traduce
> 1. Iba a llamarte, pero se me olvidó.
> 2. Va a llover; coge el paraguas.

> [!success]- Solución 4
> 1. **I was going to call you, but I forgot.** — 2. **It's going to rain; take an umbrella.**
