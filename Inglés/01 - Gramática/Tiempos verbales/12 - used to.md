<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">`used to + verbo base` describe hábitos y estados del pasado que **ya no ocurren**</mark>: cómo eran las cosas antes.

# Estructura

| Forma | Patrón | Ejemplo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Afirmativa | **used to** + base | I **used to smoke**. |
| Negativa | **didn't use to** + base | I **didn't use to like** it. |
| Interrogativa | **Did** + S + **use to** + base | **Did** you **use to live** here? |

<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">En negativa e interrogativa es `use to` (sin `-d`)</mark>, porque `did` ya marca el pasado.

# Cuándo se usa

- **Hábito pasado terminado:** *I **used to play** football when I was a kid* (ya no).
- **Estado pasado:** *There **used to be** a cinema here.*

# used to vs would vs past simple

- **`would`** también sirve para **hábitos** pasados repetidos (*We **would spend** summers at the beach*), pero <mark style="background: #8000E1A6;">`would` NO vale para estados</mark>: *There would be a cinema* ❌ → *There **used to be*** ✅.
- Para una **acción única** del pasado va el past simple, no *used to*: *I used to break my leg in 2019* ❌ → *I **broke** my leg* ✅.

# be used to / get used to (¡ojo, otra cosa!)

<mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">Son estructuras distintas y confundirlas es un error muy común</mark>:

- `used to + base` = hábito pasado.
- `be used to + -ing/nombre` = **estar acostumbrado a**: *I**'m used to working** late.*
- `get used to + -ing/nombre` = **acostumbrarse (proceso)**: *She **got used to** the cold.*

La diferencia: tras `be/get used to` va **`-ing` o un sustantivo**, no el infinitivo.

> [!warning]+ Trampas de examen ("para pillar")
> - **Negativa/pregunta sin `-d`:** *Did you used to…?* ❌ → *Did you **use** to…?* ✅.
> - **`used to` para hábitos, no para veces contadas:** si hay un número (*three times*), va past simple.
> - **`I'm used to + -ing`** (acostumbrado) ≠ **`I used to + base`** (solía). Mira lo que va detrás.

# Ejercicios

> [!question]+ 1. Rellena con used to (afirmativa, negativa o pregunta)
> 1. I ______ (have) long hair, but now it's short.
> 2. ______ there ______ (be) a shop on this corner?
> 3. He ______ (not/like) vegetables when he was little.
> 4. We ______ (go) to that café every Sunday.

> [!success]- Solución 1
> 1. **used to have** — 2. **Did** there **use to** be — 3. **didn't use to like** — 4. **used to go**

> [!question]+ 2. Elige: used to, would o past simple
> 1. There ______ be a castle here. → (a) used to (b) would
> 2. Every summer we ______ visit our grandparents. → (a) used to / would (b) — solo past simple
> 3. Yesterday I ______ my keys. → (a) used to lose (b) lost

> [!success]- Solución 2
> 1. **a** (estado → solo *used to*) — 2. **a** (hábito → ambos) — 3. **b** (una vez → past simple)

> [!question]+ 3. used to o be/get used to
> 1. It was hard at first, but now I ______ getting up early. → (I'm ______)
> 2. When I was young, I ______ eat a lot of sweets.
> 3. She can't ______ the noise of the city. (get ______)

> [!success]- Solución 3
> 1. **'m used to** (+ -ing) — 2. **used to** eat — 3. **get used to**

> [!question]+ 4. Corrige el error
> 1. Did you used to live abroad?
> 2. There would be a river here years ago.
> 3. I'm used to work at night.

> [!success]- Solución 4
> 1. Did you **use** to live abroad? — 2. There **used to** be a river… (estado) — 3. I'm used to **working** at night
