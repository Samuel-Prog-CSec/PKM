Este contraste aparece en casi todos los exámenes. La regla que resuelve el 90 % de los casos: <mark style="background: #8000E1A6;">si el periodo de tiempo está **terminado**, usa past simple; si sigue **abierto** o conecta con el ahora, usa present perfect</mark>.

| Past simple | Present perfect |
| --- | --- |
| Momento concreto y terminado | Tiempo indefinido o hasta ahora |
| *I **saw** her **yesterday**.* | *I**'ve seen** her (alguna vez).* |
| *He **lived** in Rome for a year* (ya no) | *He**'s lived** in Rome for a year* (sigue) |
| Marcadores: yesterday, ago, last, in 2010, then, when | Marcadores: ever, never, just, yet, already, since, for, so far, recently |

# El truco del marcador temporal

- ¿Momento **acabado** (`last night`, `in 2019`, `when I was 10`, `two days ago`)? → **past simple**, sí o sí.
- ¿`since / for / just / yet / already / ever / never / so far / lately`? → **present perfect**.
- ¿Preguntas **`when`**? → siempre **past simple** (*When did you arrive?*).

# La típica secuencia de examen

Se suele **presentar** una experiencia en present perfect y luego **detallar** en past simple:
*I**'ve been** to Japan. I **went** there in 2018 and **stayed** for a month.*
<mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">El primer verbo (sin fecha) va en perfect; en cuanto aparece el "cuándo", cambia a simple</mark>.

# Casos atípicos

- **`today, this week, this morning`**: si el periodo aún no ha acabado, admite present perfect (*I've drunk two coffees today*); si el momento ya pasó dentro de hoy (*this morning*, ya de tarde), past simple.

> [!warning]+ Trampas de examen ("para pillar")
> - **"Ya no ocurre" → past simple**, aunque tengas *for*: *I lived there **for** ten years* (ya no vivo). *For* no obliga a perfect; lo obliga la conexión con el presente.
> - **`just now`** (= hace un momento) va con **past simple**, aunque *just* solo vaya con perfect: *He left **just now**.*
> - Ojo con **`been` como pasado de go** (experiencia) frente a **was/were**.

# Ejercicios

> [!question]+ 1. Rellena (past simple o present perfect)
> 1. I ______ (buy) this jacket last week.
> 2. ______ you ever ______ (be) to Japan?
> 3. She ______ (not/call) me since Tuesday.
> 4. When ______ they ______ (get) married?
> 5. He ______ (live) in Paris from 2010 to 2015.

> [!success]- Solución 1
> 1. **bought** — 2. **Have** you ever **been** — 3. **hasn't called** — 4. **did** they **get** — 5. **lived** (periodo terminado)

> [!question]+ 2. Elige la opción correcta
> 1. I ______ my keys. I can't find them. → (a) lost (b) have lost
> 2. Shakespeare ______ many plays. → (a) wrote (b) has written
> 3. We ______ to that restaurant last night. → (a) have gone (b) went

> [!success]- Solución 2
> 1. **b** (resultado ahora) — 2. **a** (Shakespeare está muerto → tiempo cerrado) — 3. **b**

> [!question]+ 3. Corrige el error
> 1. I have seen that film yesterday.
> 2. Did you ever eat sushi?
> 3. She has bought a car in 2020.

> [!success]- Solución 3
> 1. I **saw** that film yesterday — 2. **Have** you ever **eaten** sushi? — 3. She **bought** a car in 2020

> [!question]+ 4. Completa el diálogo (dos tiempos seguidos)
> "______ you ever ______ (break) a bone?" "Yes, I ______ (break) my arm when I was ten."

> [!success]- Solución 4
> "**Have** you ever **broken**…?" "Yes, I **broke** my arm when I was ten."
